### 1. GATE DA 2025 — Question 11
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Multiple Choice Question (MCQ)
- **Marks:** 1
- **Difficulty:** Easy | **Concept Depth:** Level 1 (Direct Property)
- **Topic:** Conditional Expectation, Law of Total Expectation

**Question:**
Suppose $X$ and $Y$ are random variables. The conditional expectation of $X$ given $Y$ is denoted by $E[X|Y]$. Then $E[E[X|Y]]$ equals

(A) $E[X|Y]$  
(B) $\frac{E[X]}{E[Y]}$  
(C) $E[X]$  
(D) $E[Y]$  

**Answer:**
**(C) $E[X]$**

**Detailed Solution:**
By the **Law of Total Expectation** (also known as Adam's Law or the Tower Property):
$$E_Y[E_X[X | Y]] = E[X]$$

*Proof:*
$$E[E[X|Y]] = \int_{-\infty}^\infty E[X|Y=y] f_Y(y) \, dy = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left( \int_{-\infty}^\infty x \frac{f_{X,Y}(x, y)}{f_Y(y)} \, dx \right) f_Y(y) \, dy$$
$$= \int_{-\infty}^\infty x \left( \int_{-\infty}^\infty f_{X,Y}(x, y) \, dy \right) dx = \int_{-\infty}^\infty x f_X(x) \, dx = E[X]$$

### 2. GATE DA 2025 — Question 45
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Multiple Choice Question (MCQ)
- **Marks:** 2
- **Difficulty:** Easy | **Concept Depth:** Level 1 (Complement Rule)
- **Topic:** Independent Events, Complement Rule

**Question:**
A random experiment consists of throwing 100 fair dice, each die having six faces numbered 1 to 6. An event $A$ represents the set of all outcomes where at least one of the dice shows a 1. Then, $P(A) =$

(A) 0  
(B) 1  
(C) $1 - \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^{100}$  
(D) $\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^{100}$  

**Answer:**
**(C) $1 - \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^{100}$**

**Detailed Solution:**
The complement event $A^c$ is "no die shows a 1".  
For each fair die, $P(\text{not 1}) = 5/6$.  
Since the 100 dice are tossed independently:
$$P(A^c) = \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^{100}$$
By the complement rule:
$$P(A) = 1 - P(A^c) = 1 - \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^{100}$$

### 3. GATE DA 2025 — Question 19
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Multiple Choice Question (MCQ)
- **Marks:** 1
- **Difficulty:** Easy | **Concept Depth:** Level 1 (Quantile Definition)
- **Topic:** Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF), Median

**Question:**
Let $X$ be a continuous random variable whose cumulative distribution function (CDF) $F_X(x)$, for some $t$, is given as follows:
$$F_X(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & x \le t \\ \frac{x - t}{4 - t}, & t \le x \le 4 \\ 1, & x \ge 4 \end{cases}$$

If the median of $X$ is 3, then what is the value of $t$?

(A) 2  
(B) 1  
(C) -1  
(D) 0  

**Answer:**
**(A) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**
By definition of median for a continuous random variable: $F_X(\text{median}) = 0.5$.  
Here median $= 3$:
$$F_X(3) = 0.5 \implies \frac{3 - t}{4 - t} = 0.5$$
$$3 - t = 2 - 0.5t \implies 0.5t = 1 \implies t = 2$$

### 4. GATE DA 2025 — Question 21
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Multiple Choice Question (MCQ)
- **Marks:** 1
- **Difficulty:** Easy | **Concept Depth:** Level 1 (Survival Function)
- **Topic:** Exponential Distribution, Parameter Estimation

**Question:**
It is given that $P(X \ge 2) = 0.25$ for an exponentially distributed random variable $X$ with $E[X] = \frac{1}{\lambda}$, where $E[X]$ denotes the expectation of $X$. What is the value of $\lambda$?  
*(ln denotes natural logarithm)*

(A) $\ln 2$  
(B) $\ln 4$  
(C) $\ln 3$  
(D) $\ln 0.25$  

**Answer:**
**(A) $\ln 2$**

**Detailed Solution:**
For an exponential random variable $X \sim \text{Exp}(\lambda)$, the survival function is:
$$P(X \ge x) = e^{-\lambda x}$$
Given $P(X \ge 2) = 0.25$:
$$e^{-2\lambda} = \frac{1}{4} \implies e^{2\lambda} = 4 \implies 2\lambda = \ln 4 = 2\ln 2 \implies \lambda = \ln 2$$

### 5. GATE CS 2025 — Question 8

- **Difficulty:** Easy | **Concept Depth:** Level 1 (Standard Binomial Trial)
- **Topic:** Binomial Distribution, Dice Rolling

**Question:**
A fair six-faced dice, with the faces labelled ‘1’, ‘2’, ‘3’, ‘4’, ‘5’, and ‘6’, is rolled thrice. What is the probability of rolling ‘6’ exactly once?

(A) $\frac{75}{216}$  
(B) $\frac{1}{6}$  
(C) $\frac{1}{18}$  
(D) $\frac{25}{216}$  

**Answer:**
**(A) $\frac{75}{216}$**

**Detailed Solution:**
Number of trials $n = 3$.  
Probability of success (rolling '6') $p = 1/6$, failure $q = 5/6$.  
$$P(X = 1) = \binom{3}{1} p^1 q^{3-1} = 3 \times \left(\frac{1}{6}\right) \times \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^2 = 3 \times \frac{25}{216} = \frac{75}{216}$$

### 7. GATE DA 2025 — Question 10

**Question:**
The number of patients per shift ($X$) consulting Dr. Gita in her past 100 shifts is shown in the figure. If the amount she earns is ₹ $1000(X - 0.2)$, what is the average amount (in ₹) she has earned per shift in the past 100 shifts?

*Note: The figure shown is representative.*

```
 Number of shifts
      50 |
         |                 40
      40 |                 |
         |                 |        30
      30 |                 |        |
         |        20       |        |
      20 |        |        |        |        10
         |        |        |        |        |

         |        |        |        |        |
```

(A) 6,100  
(B) 6,300  
(C) 6,000

### 8. GATE DA 2025 — Question 20
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Multiple Choice Question (MCQ)
- **Marks:** 1
- **Difficulty:** Medium | **Concept Depth:** Level 2 (Normal Linear Moment System)
- **Topic:** Normal Distribution, Linear Transformation, Covariance

**Question:**
Let $X = aZ + b$, where $Z$ is a standard normal random variable, and $a, b$ are two unknown constants. It is given that
$$E[X] = 1, \quad E[(X - E[X])Z] = -2, \quad E[(X - E[X])^2] = 4,$$
where $E[X]$ denotes the expectation of random variable $X$. The values of $a, b$ are:

(A) $a = -2, \; b = 1$  
(B) $a = 2, \; b = -1$  
(C) $a = -2, \; b = -1$  
(D) $a = 1, \; b = 1$  

**Answer:**
**(A) $a = -2, \; b = 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**
Since $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \implies E[Z] = 0, \; E[Z^2] = 1$.
1. $E[X] = aE[Z] + b = b = 1 \implies b = 1$.
2. $X - E[X] = (aZ + 1) - 1 = aZ$.
3. $E[(X - E[X])Z] = E[(aZ)Z] = aE[Z^2] = a(1) = a$.  
   Given $E[(X - E[X])Z] = -2 \implies a = -2$.
4. Check variance: $E[(aZ)^2] = a^2 E[Z^2] = (-2)^2(1) = 4$ (Matches).

Thus, $a = -2$ and $b = 1$.

### 10. GATE DA 2025 — Question 31
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Numerical Answer Type (NAT)
- **Marks:** 1
- **Difficulty:** Medium | **Concept Depth:** Level 2 (Multi-Branch Bayes' Inversion)
- **Topic:** Bayes' Theorem, Law of Total Probability

**Question:**
There are three boxes containing white balls and black balls.
- Box-1 contains 2 black and 1 white balls.
- Box-2 contains 1 black and 2 white balls.
- Box-3 contains 3 black and 3 white balls.

In a random experiment, one of these boxes is selected, where the probability of choosing Box-1 is $\frac{1}{2}$, Box-2 is $\frac{1}{6}$, and Box-3 is $\frac{1}{3}$. A ball is drawn at random from the selected box. Given that the ball drawn is white, the probability that it is drawn from Box-2 is ______ (Round off to two decimal places).

**Answer:**
**0.25** (Answer range: 0.25 to 0.25)

**Detailed Solution:**
- $P(B_1) = 1/2, \; P(B_2) = 1/6, \; P(B_3) = 1/3$.
- Likelihood of white ball:
  $$P(W | B_1) = \frac{1}{3}, \quad P(W | B_2) = \frac{2}{3}, \quad P(W | B_3) = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$$
1. Total probability:
   $$P(W) = \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) + \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)\left(\frac{1}{6}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{2}{18} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{8}{18} = \frac{4}{9}$$
2. Bayes' posterior for Box-2:
   $$P(B_2 | W) = \frac{P(W | B_2)P(B_2)}{P(W)} = \frac{\frac{2}{18}}{\frac{8}{18}} = \frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25$$

### 11. GATE DA 2025 — Question 36
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Multiple Choice Question (MCQ)
- **Marks:** 2
- **Difficulty:** Medium | **Concept Depth:** Level 2 (Distributional Transformation)
- **Topic:** Normal Distribution, Chi-Squared Distribution

**Question:**
Let $Y = Z^2, \; Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}$, where $X$ is a normal random variable with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$. The variance of $Y$ is

(A) 1  
(B) 2  
(C) 3  
(D) 4  

**Answer:**
**(B) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**
Standardizing $X$:
$$Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$$
Then $Y = Z^2$ is the square of a standard normal random variable, which follows a Chi-squared distribution with 1 degree of freedom:
$$Y \sim \chi^2(k=1)$$
For a Chi-squared distribution $\chi^2(k)$, the variance is $2k$.  
With $k = 1$:
$$\text{Var}(Y) = 2(1) = 2$$

### 12. GATE DA 2025 — Question 39
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Multiple Choice Question (MCQ)
- **Marks:** 2
- **Difficulty:** Medium | **Concept Depth:** Level 2 (Piecewise CDF Interval Evaluation)
- **Topic:** Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF), Probability Calculation

**Question:**
Consider the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of a random variable $X$:
$$F_X(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & x \le -1 \\ \frac{1}{4}(x + 1)^2, & -1 \le x \le 1 \\ 1, & x \ge 1 \end{cases}$$

The value of $P(X^2 \le 0.25)$ is

(A) 0.625  
(B) 0.25  
(C) 0.5  
(D) 0.5625  

**Answer:**
**(C) 0.5**

**Detailed Solution:**
$$X^2 \le 0.25 \iff -0.5 \le X \le 0.5$$
For a continuous random variable:
$$P(-0.5 \le X \le 0.5) = F_X(0.5) - F_X(-0.5)$$
Evaluating $F_X(x) = \frac{1}{4}(x+1)^2$:
- $F_X(0.5) = \frac{1}{4}(1.5)^2 = \frac{2.25}{4} = 0.5625$
- $F_X(-0.5) = \frac{1}{4}(0.5)^2 = \frac{0.25}{4} = 0.0625$
$$P(X^2 \le 0.25) = 0.5625 - 0.0625 = 0.50$$

### 13. GATE DA 2025 — Question 61
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Numerical Answer Type (NAT)
- **Marks:** 2
- **Difficulty:** Medium | **Concept Depth:** Level 2 (Sampling with Replacement)
- **Topic:** Binomial Distribution, Expectation

**Question:**
A bag contains 5 white balls and 10 black balls. In a random experiment, $n$ balls are drawn from the bag one at a time with replacement. Let $S_n$ denote the total number of black balls drawn in the experiment.  
The expectation of $S_{100}$ denoted by $E[S_{100}] =$ ______ (Round off to one decimal place).

**Answer:**
**66.7** (Answer range: 66.6 to 66.8)

**Detailed Solution:**
Total balls $= 15$. Sampling is performed **with replacement**, so each draw is an independent Bernoulli trial with probability of black ball:
$$p = \frac{10}{15} = \frac{2}{3}$$
For $n = 100$ draws: $S_{100} \sim \text{Binomial}(100, 2/3)$.  
$$E[S_{100}] = n \cdot p = 100 \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{200}{3} \approx 66.6667 \approx 66.7$$

### 14. GATE CS 2025 — Question 58
- **Source:** GATE 2025 CS Official Paper (Session 1)
- **Section:** Computer Science and Information Technology (CS)
- **Question Type:** Numerical Answer Type (NAT)
- **Marks:** 2
- **Difficulty:** Medium | **Concept Depth:** Level 2 (Density Normalization & Integration)
- **Topic:** Continuous Random Variable, Probability Density Function

**Question:**
Consider a probability distribution given by the density function $P(x)$:
$$P(x) = \begin{cases} Cx^2, & \text{for } 1 \le x \le 4 \\ 0, & \text{for } x < 1 \text{ or } x > 4 \end{cases}$$
The probability that $x$ lies between 2 and 3, i.e., $P(2 \le x \le 3)$ is __________. (rounded off to three decimal places)

**Answer:**
**0.302** (Answer range: 0.300 to 0.302)

**Detailed Solution:**
1. Find $C$:
   $$\int_1^4 C x^2 \, dx = 1 \implies C \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_1^4 = C \left( \frac{64 - 1}{3} \right) = 21 C = 1 \implies C = \frac{1}{21}$$
2. Calculate $P(2 \le x \le 3)$:
   $$P(2 \le x \le 3) = \int_2^3 \frac{1}{21} x^2 \, dx = \frac{1}{21} \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_2^3 = \frac{1}{21} \times \frac{27 - 8}{3} = \frac{19}{63} \approx 0.301587 \approx 0.302$$

### 15. GATE CS 2025 — Question 32
- **Source:** GATE 2025 CS Official Paper (Session 1)
- **Section:** Computer Science and Information Technology (CS)
- **Question Type:** Numerical Answer Type (NAT)
- **Marks:** 1
- **Difficulty:** Medium | **Concept Depth:** Level 2 (Multi-Step Bayes Update)
- **Topic:** Bayes' Theorem, Conditional Probability

**Question:**
A box contains 5 coins: 4 regular coins and 1 fake coin. When a regular coin is tossed, the probability $P(\text{head}) = 0.5$ and for a fake coin, $P(\text{head}) = 1$. You pick a coin at random and toss it twice, and get two heads. The probability that the coin you have chosen is the fake coin is _______. (rounded off to two decimal places)

**Answer:**
**0.50** (Answer range: 0.49 to 0.51)

**Detailed Solution:**
Priors: $P(\text{Fake}) = 1/5 = 0.2, \; P(\text{Regular}) = 4/5 = 0.8$.  
Likelihood of two heads ($HH$):
- $P(HH | \text{Fake}) = 1 \times 1 = 1.0$.
- $P(HH | \text{Regular}) = 0.5 \times 0.5 = 0.25$.
Total Probability:
$$P(HH) = (1.0 \times 0.2) + (0.25 \times 0.8) = 0.2 + 0.2 = 0.4$$
Posterior:
$$P(\text{Fake} | HH) = \frac{P(HH | \text{Fake}) P(\text{Fake})}{P(HH)} = \frac{0.2}{0.4} = 0.50$$

### 16. GATE DA 2025 — Question 35
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Numerical Answer Type (NAT)
- **Marks:** 1
- **Difficulty:** Hard | **Concept Depth:** Level 3 (Bayes Optimal Classifier Error Rate)
- **Topic:** Naïve Bayes Classifier, Misclassification Probability

**Question:**
The naive Bayes classifier is used to solve a two-class classification problem with class-labels $y_1, y_2$. Suppose the prior probabilities are $P(y_1) = \frac{1}{3}$ and $P(y_2) = \frac{2}{3}$. Assuming a discrete feature space with
$$P(x | y_1) = \frac{3}{4} \quad \text{and} \quad P(x | y_2) = \frac{1}{4}$$
for a specific feature vector $x$. The probability of misclassifying $x$ is ______ (Round off to two decimal places).

**Answer:**
**0.40** (Answer range: 0.39 to 0.41)

**Detailed Solution:**
1. Joint probabilities:
   $$P(x, y_1) = P(x | y_1) P(y_1) = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25$$
   $$P(x, y_2) = P(x | y_2) P(y_2) = \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6} \approx 0.1667$$
2. Total probability $P(x) = \frac{3}{12} + \frac{2}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$.
3. Posterior probabilities:
   $$P(y_1 | x) = \frac{3/12}{5/12} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.60, \quad P(y_2 | x) = \frac{2/12}{5/12} = \frac{2}{5} = 0.40$$
4. Decision Rule: The Bayes classifier selects the class with higher posterior: $\hat{y} = y_1$ (since $0.60 > 0.40$).
5. Misclassification error occurs when the true class was $y_2$:
   $$P(\text{error} | x) = P(y_2 | x) = \frac{2}{5} = 0.40$$

### 17. GATE DA 2025 — Question 40
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Multiple Choice Question (MCQ)
- **Marks:** 2
- **Difficulty:** Hard | **Concept Depth:** Level 3 (Asymptotic Normal Approximation / CLT)
- **Topic:** Bernoulli Distribution, Central Limit Theorem

**Question:**
A random variable $X$ is said to be distributed as $\text{Bernoulli}(\theta)$, denoted by $X \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\theta)$, if
$$P(X = 1) = \theta, \quad P(X = 0) = 1 - \theta$$
for $0 < \theta < 1$. Let $Y = \sum_{i=1}^{300} X_i$, where $X_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\theta), \; i = 1, 2, \dots, 300$ be independent and identically distributed random variables with $\theta = 0.25$. The value of $P(60 \le Y \le 90)$, after approximation through Central Limit Theorem, is given by
$$\text{Recall that } \Phi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^x e^{-\frac{t^2}{2}} \, dt$$

(A) $\Phi(2) - \Phi(-2)$  
(B) $\Phi(1) - \Phi(-1)$  
(C) $\Phi(3) - \Phi(-3)$  
(D) $\Phi(90) - \Phi(60)$  

**Answer:**
**(A) $\Phi(2) - \Phi(-2)$**

**Detailed Solution:**
For each $X_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\theta=0.25)$:
$$E[X_i] = \theta = 0.25, \quad \text{Var}(X_i) = \theta(1-\theta) = 0.25 \times 0.75 = \frac{3}{16} = 0.1875$$
For the sum $Y = \sum_{i=1}^{300} X_i$:
$$\mu_Y = n\theta = 300 \times 0.25 = 75$$
$$\sigma_Y^2 = n\theta(1-\theta) = 300 \times \frac{3}{16} = 56.25 \implies \sigma_Y = \sqrt{56.25} = 7.5$$
By CLT, $Y \approx \mathcal{N}(75, 7.5^2)$.  
Standardizing the endpoints:
$$z_1 = \frac{60 - 75}{7.5} = \frac{-15}{7.5} = -2$$
$$z_2 = \frac{90 - 75}{7.5} = \frac{15}{7.5} = 2$$
$$P(60 \le Y \le 90) = \Phi(2) - \Phi(-2)$$

### 18. GATE DA 2025 — Question 41
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Multiple Choice Question (MCQ)
- **Marks:** 2
- **Difficulty:** Hard | **Concept Depth:** Level 3 (Non-linear Functional Transformation)
- **Topic:** Exponential Distribution, Floor Transformation, Geometric Distribution

**Question:**
For $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the floor function is denoted by $f(x) = \lfloor x \rfloor$ and defined as follows
$$\lfloor x \rfloor = k, \quad k \le x < k + 1,$$
where $k$ is an integer. Let $Y = \lfloor X \rfloor$, where $X$ is an exponentially distributed random variable with mean $\frac{1}{\ln 10}$, where $\ln$ denotes natural logarithm. For any positive integer $\ell$, one can write the probability of the event $Y = \ell$ as follows
$$P(Y = \ell) = q^\ell (1 - q)$$

The value of $q$ is

(A) 0.1  
(B) 0.01  
(C) 0.5  
(D) 0.434  

**Answer:**
**(A) 0.1**

**Detailed Solution:**
Given mean $1/\lambda = 1/\ln 10 \implies \lambda = \ln 10$.  
Survival function for $X \sim \text{Exp}(\lambda)$ is $P(X \ge x) = e^{-\lambda x}$.  
For positive integer $\ell$:
$$Y = \lfloor X \rfloor = \ell \iff \ell \le X < \ell + 1$$
$$P(Y = \ell) = P(X \ge \ell) - P(X \ge \ell + 1) = e^{-\lambda \ell} - e^{-\lambda(\ell + 1)} = e^{-\lambda \ell} (1 - e^{-\lambda}) = (e^{-\lambda})^\ell (1 - e^{-\lambda})$$
Matching with the given Geometric form $P(Y = \ell) = q^\ell (1 - q)$:
$$q = e^{-\lambda} = e^{-\ln 10} = \frac{1}{10} = 0.1$$

### 19. GATE DA 2025 — Question 54
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Multiple Select Question (MSQ)
- **Marks:** 2
- **Difficulty:** Hard | **Concept Depth:** Level 3 (Mathematical Statistics & Asymptotics)
- **Topic:** Estimation Theory, Sample Mean, Unbiasedness & Variance

**Question:**
Consider a coin-toss experiment where the probability of head showing up is $p$. In the $i^{\text{th}}$ coin toss, let $X_i = 1$ if head appears, and $X_i = 0$ if tail appears. Consider
$$\hat{p} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$$
where $n$ is the total number of independent coin tosses.

Which of the following statements is/are correct?

(A) $E[\hat{p}] = p$  
(B) $E[\hat{p}] = \frac{p}{n}$  
(C) As $n$ increases, variance of $\hat{p}$ decreases  
(D) Variance of $\hat{p}$ does not depend on $n$  

**Answer:**
**(A) and (C)**

**Detailed Solution:**
The indicator variables $X_1, \dots, X_n$ are i.i.d. $\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ with mean $E[X_i] = p$ and variance $\text{Var}(X_i) = p(1-p)$.
1. **Unbiasedness:**
   $$E[\hat{p}] = E\left[ \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \right] = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n E[X_i] = \frac{n p}{n} = p$$
   $\implies$ Statement (A) is **CORRECT**, (B) is **INCORRECT**.
2. **Variance Asymptotics:**
   $$\text{Var}(\hat{p}) = \text{Var}\left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \right) = \frac{1}{n^2} \sum_{i=1}^n \text{Var}(X_i) = \frac{n p(1-p)}{n^2} = \frac{p(1-p)}{n}$$
   Since $\text{Var}(\hat{p}) \propto 1/n$, as sample size $n$ increases, the variance decreases to 0.  
   $\implies$ Statement (C) is **CORRECT**, (D) is **INCORRECT**.

### 20. GATE DA 2025 — Question 60
- **Source:** GATE 2025 DA Official Paper
- **Section:** Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (DA)
- **Question Type:** Numerical Answer Type (NAT)
- **Marks:** 2
- **Difficulty:** Hard | **Concept Depth:** Level 3 (Spectral Theory & Rayleigh Quotient)
- **Topic:** Sample Covariance Matrix, Direction of Maximum Variance (PCA)

**Question:**
Let $D = \{x^{(1)}, \dots, x^{(n)}\}$ be a dataset of $n$ observations where each $x^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{100}$. It is given that $\sum_{i=1}^n x^{(i)} = 0$. The covariance matrix computed from $D$ has eigenvalues $\lambda_i = 100^{2-i}, \; 1 \le i \le 100$. Let $u \in \mathbb{R}^{100}$ be the direction of maximum variance with $u^T u = 1$.

The value of
$$\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n (u^T x^{(i)})^2 = \text{______}$$
*(Answer in integer)*

**Answer:**
**100** (Answer range: 100 to 100)

**Detailed Solution:**
Since the dataset is zero-mean ($\sum x^{(i)} = 0$), the sample covariance matrix $\Sigma$ is:
$$\Sigma = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x^{(i)} (x^{(i)})^T$$
The variance along any unit vector $u$ ($u^T u = 1$) is given by the quadratic form:
$$\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n (u^T x^{(i)})^2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n u^T x^{(i)} (x^{(i)})^T u = u^T \left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x^{(i)} (x^{(i)})^T \right) u = u^T \Sigma u$$

By the Rayleigh-Ritz theorem:
$$\max_{u: u^T u = 1} u^T \Sigma u = \lambda_{\max}$$
The given eigenvalues are $\lambda_i = 100^{2-i}$ for $i = 1, \dots, 100$:
- $\lambda_1 = 100^{2-1} = 100^1 = 100$
- $\lambda_2 = 100^{2-2} = 100^0 = 1$
- $\lambda_3 = 100^{-1} = 0.01$

The maximum eigenvalue is $\lambda_{\max} = \lambda_1 = 100$.  
Therefore:
$$\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n (u^T x^{(i)})^2 = 100$$
